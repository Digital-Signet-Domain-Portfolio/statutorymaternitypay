Contractual or enhanced maternity pay

Edited by Oliver Wakefield-Smith, Founder of Digital Signet. Last reviewed 23 June 2026.

Direct answer

What is enhanced maternity pay?

Enhanced or contractual maternity pay is anything your employer pays above the statutory SMP. It is governed by your contract or staff handbook, not by statute. Common patterns: full pay for 6 to 18 weeks, then half pay plus SMP for a further block, then SMP only. Many enhanced schemes include a return-to-work clawback clause requiring you to repay if you leave within a defined window.

Typical patterns

  • Public sector and large private employers: full pay for 18 to 26 weeks, then half pay plus SMP for 13 weeks, then SMP only.
  • Mid-market private: full pay for 6 to 12 weeks, then SMP only.
  • Statutory minimum: SMP only.
  • Tech: increasingly competitive: full pay for 16 to 26 weeks is common at series-B and above.

Interaction with SMP

Enhanced pay sits on top of SMP, not instead of it. Payroll processes SMP through the FPS as normal, then adds the contractual top-up. The SMP element is the only part you can reclaim from HMRC through the EPS.

Return-to-work clawback

Many contracts require repayment of the enhanced element if you do not return to work for a defined period, often 3 to 6 months after leave ends. The SMP element cannot be clawed back; only the enhanced portion is repayable. Tribunal case law has held that a reasonably-drafted clawback is enforceable.

Sectors most likely to enhance

CIPD survey data shows the public sector, education, financial services and large technology firms most commonly offer enhanced maternity pay. Hospitality, retail and small business are most likely to pay SMP only.
